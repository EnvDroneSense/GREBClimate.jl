Reading the plots

Absolute values and anomalies

The control run is drawn in absolute values on the viridis scale. A scenario that is an anomaly (scnr_anomaly is true in the result) is the change against the control and is drawn on a scale centred at zero: red is warmer or more, blue colder or less, white no change. A scenario that is not an anomaly, such as an orbital run, is drawn in absolute values. A result without scnr_anomaly, and a bare record vector, count as absolute: the plots do not guess.

Panel titles say which: scenario anomaly - Surface temperature.

Colour scales

PlotScale
plot_map, plot_hovmollerSet by the data of each panel; the control and the scenario do not share a scale
plot_differenceSymmetric about zero, set by the largest difference
AnimationsFixed for all frames of a run: the same colour is the same value in every frame
Anomaly animationsSymmetric about zero at the 99th percentile of the absolute values, so that a few cells with large changes do not wash out the rest. Those cells saturate

Compare panels by their colour bar, not by colour alone.

Means and weights

A global mean weights each cell by cos(latitude), because cells near the poles cover less area. A plain average over the grid counts every row equally and reads about 8.5 to 10.6 K too cold for the control surface temperature. All the plots and series use the weighted mean; masks keep the weights inside the region.

What a 96x48 grid can show

FeatureWhat to expect
Cell size3.75 by 3.75 degrees. Features smaller than a few cells, such as a lake or a mountain range, are not resolved
CoastlinesThe outline follows cell edges: land is where the model's topography is above 0 m, so small islands and straits are missing or wide
PolesThe first and last rows are bands near the poles. Zonal means there average over short circles
SmoothnessMaps are drawn cell by cell, not smoothed. Sharp edges in a map are cell edges

Where a signal comes from

QuestionPlot to use
Where did it change?plot_map of the scenario anomaly, or plot_difference of two runs
How much, at which latitude?plot_zonal
When in the year?plot_seasonal, or plot_hovmoller for latitude and season together
How fast, and has it settled?plot_timeseries with annual = true: a flat line at the end means the run has reached a new balance, a rising line that it has not
Which process matters?Run the model with the process switched off and use plot_compare or plot_members (see examples/feedback_deconstruction.jl)

Limits to keep in mind

  • The control run drifts little by design: the model is flux-corrected against the climatology during the spin-up. A flat control line is expected, not a sign of an error.
  • A short scenario shows the fast response. After 15 years the warming is still rising (see the annual time series); plot_timeseries shows how much is left.
  • Each member of an ensemble with different physics needs its own spin-up before its output means anything.
  • The viz plots show model output; they do not test it. A plausible-looking map can still be wrong, so the run checks of the package (RangeCheck) come first.
